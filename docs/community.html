Clearing the noise, not the mystery

People have watched lights over Mitchell Flat for more than 140 years. Some of what they saw has never been explained, including an experience of the author's own. This project isn't here to explain those experiences away. It removes the noise that anyone can see, such as headlights, trains, towers and towns, so that the experiences left over can be looked at carefully and taken seriously.

Under one of the darkest skies in North America

The dark-sky country around Marfa

The Viewing Area sits inside the Greater Big Bend International Dark Sky Reserve. The dark skies that make the Milky Way so vivid here also let a single car 40 km away shine like a bright star. That is why a careful map of every light matters so much in this place.

Since 2022 · over 15,000 sq mi

Greater Big Bend International Dark Sky Reserve

When it was certified in spring 2022, it became the largest certified Dark Sky Place in the world. It spans Brewster, Jeff Davis, Presidio and Reeves counties and crosses into Mexico. Its core areas are McDonald Observatory and the Nature Conservancy's Davis Mountains Preserve. The reserve reports that every community and county within it has adopted an outdoor lighting ordinance.1,2

McDonald Observatory

The University of Texas observatory runs public star parties: an open-air constellation tour with telescope views. They often sell out, so book ahead. Its Dark Skies Initiative monitors light pollution in Far West Texas and advises property owners and businesses on night-sky friendly lighting.3,4

Big Bend National Park

The National Park Service describes Big Bend as having the least light pollution of any park unit in the lower 48 states. On a clear night you can see about 2,000 stars there, against a few hundred in a mid-sized city.5,6

On the skyline you are watching

Big Bend Ranch State Park and the Chinati Mountains

Big Bend Ranch State Park became an International Dark Sky Park in 2017. In 2020 the Chinati Mountains State Natural Area joined that designation. Together they protect about 355,000 acres of dark sky. The Chinati Mountains form the far skyline you see from the Viewing Area. When the 2020 designation was announced, the natural area was not yet open to the public.7

Marfa

A town that lives with its lights

The Marfa Lights belong to Marfa. The Marfa Visitor Center points visitors to the official Viewing Area, 9 miles east of town on US-90 toward Alpine.8 Each Labor Day weekend the Marfa Chamber of Commerce holds the Marfa Lights Festival around the Presidio County Courthouse. The 38th festival ran from 29 to 31 August 2025.9

If you come to watch, spend your days in town too. Eat locally, visit the Visitor Center, and leave the town and the ranches as you found them.

Being a good neighbour at the Viewing Area

  • Stay on public ground. Mitchell Flat and the land around the Viewing Area are private working ranches. Never cross fences or go looking for the lights on foot or by car.
  • Keep it dark. Switch off headlights once parked. Use red light only; this site has a red night-vision mode. Keep phone screens dim. Other people are dark-adapting too.
  • Don't stop on the highway. Pull fully into the Viewing Area. US-90 is a fast road at night.
  • Be kind to other watchers. Talk quietly. Ask before shining anything toward someone's camera.
  • Write it down before you talk about it. Comparing notes too early makes people's memories converge, and then two witnesses count as one. Record your own account first.10

If you live or stay here: light kindly

Every unshielded yard light on the flat is a new light for observers to rule out, and a little less of the sky for everyone. DarkSky International's five principles for outdoor lighting:11

  • UsefulUse light only if it is needed.
  • TargetedDirect light only where it is needed.
  • Low levelNo brighter than necessary.
  • ControlledUse light only when it is needed.
  • Warm-colouredUse warmer colours where possible.

McDonald Observatory's Dark Skies Initiative advises property owners in the region on night-sky friendly lighting.4

Share what you see

Report a sighting

If you see a light you can't explain, a careful report is the most valuable thing you can give the investigation. The report form walks you through what to note: time, direction, height against the skyline, colour, motion and conditions. It checks your light against every known source, so you can see straight away whether it falls where ordinary lights are expected. Your report stays on your device until you choose to send it.
