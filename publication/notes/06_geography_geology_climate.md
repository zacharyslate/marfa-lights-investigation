# 6. Setting: geography, geology and climate

These notes give only the context the analysis relies on. Each statement is either sourced or comes from this project's own measurements, and is marked as such.

## Place

- **Marfa.** Presidio County seat, at about 4,700 ft (1,430 m). It was founded in the early 1880s as a railroad water stop. The 2020 census counted 1,788 residents [1].
- **The Marfa Mystery Lights Viewing Area** is on US-90, about 9 miles east of Marfa [9]. The ground there is at 1,493.6 m (3DEP). From the platform, the observer looks south-southwest to west across Mitchell Flat, which is private ranchland.
- **US-67** runs from Marfa south-southwest to Shafter and Presidio.
  - It climbs to a high point of about 1,650 m, at 228.9° true and about 40 km from the platform (this project, 3DEP).
  - It then descends past Shafter, a former silver-mining town [5].
- **RM 2810 (Pinto Canyon Road)** leaves Marfa to the south-west. It climbs into the Chinati foothills and, measured from the platform, is in view for 27 km at 253°–259° true (this project).
- **Railroads.**
  - The Union Pacific Sunset Route runs beside US-90, past the platform.
  - The Texas Pacifico line (the former South Orient) runs south to Presidio. The track is in view 3.8–8.1 km from the platform (this project).

## Terrain seen from the platform

- **The far skyline is the Chinati Mountains, 60–70 km away.**
  - Chinati Peak, at 2,355 m (7,728 ft), is the highest point in Presidio County [2].
  - It stands at 238.3° true and 67.5 km (this project).
- **Low rises in the foreground hide most of US-67.** Figure 4 shows this: at 240° the road lies behind the Mitchell Flat rise.

## Geology

- **The Chinati Mountains are the eroded remains of a caldera complex in the Trans-Pecos volcanic field [3, 4].**
  - The Chinati Mountains caldera erupted about 32–31 million years ago. It produced the Mitchell Mesa Tuff.
  - An older caldera, the Infiernito, formed about 37 million years ago [3, 4].
- **Relevance to the lights.** Geological explanations of the lights are sometimes proposed, for example piezoelectric or other "earth lights" hypotheses. This project does not assess them. Nothing in the geometric analysis depends on them.

## Climate and sky

- **Climate.** Marfa has a semi-arid steppe climate, with about 15.4 in. (390 mm) of rain a year. The mean daily maximum is 76.5 °F and the minimum 42.8 °F [1, citing Western Regional Climate Center normals].
- **Why that matters.** A large day–night temperature range and dry, clear, calm nights are the conditions that produce strong surface temperature inversions, and so large positive refraction coefficients (note 2).
- **Dark sky.** In April 2022 the Greater Big Bend region became an International Dark Sky Reserve, the first to cross an international border [6]. The resulting low artificial skyglow is why distant headlights and ranch lights stand out from the platform.

## Local history relevant to lights on the flat

- **Marfa Army Air Field** (1942–45) occupied the flat beside today's Viewing Area. It had a main field and four auxiliary fields [7].
- **The Handbook of Texas** summarises the traditional sightings back to 1883 and the folklore around them [8]. The historical and cultural material is covered in the website's *History and folklore* page and will be written up in the manuscript.
